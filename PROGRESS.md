### T9.55e — a replayed GET waits until the closing connections have closed

- Cause: `selftest/stack.spec.ts:53` ("every request handed to the stack as its pooled connections idle out is answered at once") failed `GET after 4976 ms idle: no answer in 3 s` in 6 of 103 WebKit and 1 of 103 Chromium shards of the three full lanes with slow legs. The GET waited in `wrangler dev`'s proxy and was released about 8 s later by the next burst: wrangler's `requestRetryQueue`. The Worker closes the pooled connections of one burst together 5 s after their last use; the patch replayed a lost GET at once onto the next pooled connection, which was closing too, and after four lost attempts the GET was parked until the next request.
- Fix: `patches/wrangler@4.113.0.patch` makes a replay wait the way a POST already does: while the newest pooled connection is 4.5 s old or more, it waits until that connection is past its idle close. The pool hands out the newest first, so a loss on it means every other pooled connection is closing as well, and one replay is then enough. The three replays, the selftest and its 3 s bound are unchanged. `scripts/wrangler-proxy.test.mjs` has the case (six connections idling out together, one GET lost, answered on one replay, nothing parked).
- Aimed measurement (one GET landing 4,988 to 4,994 ms after six connections were used together, 282 GETs per rule in both engines): with the replay sent at once, 62 first attempts lost and 18 GETs unanswered in 3 s; with the wait, 76 lost and none unanswered, each answered on the first replay after a wait of 300 to 306 ms.
- Cost: a GET whose first forward is lost is answered about 0.3 s later (at most 0.8 s by the patch's constants). A lost WebSocket upgrade is a GET and waits the same. In a lane on the fix about nine selftest GETs per shard took 0.3 s, so "answered at once" in the selftest's title includes them.
- What is left: the wait fires only when the proxy's model of its pool says the newest connection is 4.5 s old or more, so a model that disagrees with the real pool replays at once as before; a GET that still loses four attempts is still parked.

### T9.55b — a note's updated time is the time of its last edit

- Cause: `Projections.touch()` writes `docs.updated_at` at a note's first edit, then at most every 5 s; its trailing write read the clock when its timer fired, so it stamped "first edit plus 5 s" whatever the last edit's time. With two notes written back to back, the first one's trailing stamp was later than the second's edits: about 5 s after its first keystroke it jumped above the second in the newest-first list, and the second's own trailing stamp put it back 1 to 3 s later. A click on a row at the instant of a jump opened nothing; that is how the wiki-links leg of `j12-search` failed in WebKit (3 of 139 full-group runs).
- Experiment, the row swap is what loses the open: the target's trailing refetch held, a press on the source's row, the refetch let through, the release. Unchanged: Chromium 5 held, the rows changed places under the press each time and the source never opened; WebKit 4 held, the same 4 of 4, and 1 execution whose held refetch never answered after it was let through (counted apart). With the fix: Chromium 5 held, the rows stayed and the source opened 5 of 5; WebKit 3 held, the same 3 of 3, and 2 executions whose refetch never answered (counted apart). Runs 45675574803 and 45675579393.
- Fix: the trailing write carries the time of the last edit. The writes still happen at once and then at most every 5 s, and the D1 write is still `MAX(updated_at, ?)`, so a stamp never goes backwards.
