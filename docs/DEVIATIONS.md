| 22 | A markdown line keeps its whole text as literal text (every character kept, escapes decoded as moss decodes a line, so export and re-import give it back unchanged, and nothing in it depends on where a budget ran out) instead of converting, when it passes a cap fixed by its own text alone: longer than 128 KiB (it then also skips moss's import normalization and the block transformers, and imports as a paragraph line), more than 6,000 matches, more than 4,096 tabs, more work than 1,800 units per byte of the line plus 256K, or more than 100M units in all. A unit is about a nanosecond or two of workerd CPU; a converted match costs a base plus 4,096 a node it makes, a tab node 6,144, each delimiter run the format search finds 512, a linear-match pre-scan run again on a text's rest 64 a character, and moss's link callback a node per delimiter character of its label before it runs (it makes a node per formatted run). The per-byte cap sits just above what a palette of colors, the densest ordinary text, takes, so a line dense in short links (`[a](b) `), short wiki links, short emphasis or code spans or tabs stays literal past a few hundred bytes, and deep emphasis nesting past about a kilobyte, where moss converts them (more slowly). These caps hold one line of any content to 250 ms of workerd CPU to import or export, and a 2 MB note of any such lines within SP2's 5 s (measured for every converter case from 1 KB to 2 MB, just under each cut, and as 2 MB notes of each case's costliest lines). Over one import, the work that grows faster than its line in moss's own import (moss callbacks that read the whole paragraph per match) is held to a budget too, each line paying only what passes its own allowance. Ordinary notes (the fixtures, the ordinary-note profiles, the 2 MB scale note and 2 MB notes of short link-dense paragraphs or of palettes) pass no cap and stay byte-identical to moss | T3.S4; SP2 | pending |
